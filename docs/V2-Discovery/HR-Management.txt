## Wendo Coffee Bistro — HR Management Module (V2)

### Current State

HR management at Wendo is handled entirely offline. There is no centralised system for tracking staff performance, recording incidents, issuing warnings, or identifying top performers. Decisions about staff — disciplinary action, recognition, promotion — are made based on the memory and judgment of branch leadership rather than documented evidence. As the business scales to more branches, this approach becomes increasingly unreliable and exposes management to making consequential HR decisions without an adequate paper trail.

The V1 system lays some groundwork — it tracks clock-in and clock-out times, records shift attendance, and captures order-level data per staff member. The V2 HR module builds on this foundation and adds the human management layer on top of it.

---

### Staff Performance Tracking

The HR manager wants visibility into how individual staff members are performing across measurable dimensions. For operational staff, meaningful performance data already flows through the system naturally — waiters handle orders, chefs prepare tickets, baristas fulfil drinks orders. The V2 module surfaces this data in a structured, per-staff view that HR and branch management can review over time.

Performance visibility is not limited to numbers. The module also needs to capture qualitative assessments from branch leadership — specifically the Head Chef, Assistant Chef, Head Waiter, and Assistant Waiter, who are closest to the day-to-day conduct and attitude of their teams and are best placed to provide structured feedback about individual staff members.

---

### Branch Leadership Feedback — Supervisory Assessments

Each branch has a defined supervisory layer: a Head Chef and Assistant Chef overseeing kitchen and barista staff, and a Head Waiter and Assistant Waiter overseeing front-of-house staff. One of the explicit responsibilities of these roles is to observe and report on the staff they supervise.

In V2, these supervisors will have access to structured assessment forms within the system — a straightforward way to submit observations about a staff member's conduct, attitude, punctuality, teamwork, and quality of work. These assessments are not freeform — they follow a defined structure to ensure consistency and usefulness across branches. Submitted assessments are attached to the relevant staff member's record and are visible to branch managers and the HR manager.

The exact format of these forms — what dimensions are assessed, how frequently assessments are expected, and whether they are routine or event-triggered — will be defined during V2 scoping.

---

### Disciplinary Records — Warnings and Incidents

The HR manager wants a formal, documented record of staff misconduct and disciplinary action within the system. When a staff member receives a verbal or written warning, that event is recorded against their profile — including the date, the nature of the issue, who issued the warning, and any supporting notes or context.

Over time, a staff member's disciplinary record builds into a clear, chronological history. This gives management the evidence base they need to make informed decisions — whether that is further disciplinary action, a performance improvement plan, or termination — without relying on memory or informal records. It also protects the business by ensuring that disciplinary decisions are traceable and documented.

Warnings follow a standard progression — verbal warning, first written warning, final written warning — though the system should accommodate situations that do not follow a linear path. Each entry in the record is permanent and timestamped. The HR manager and branch manager can view a staff member's full disciplinary history. The staff member themselves may or may not have visibility into their own record — this will be decided during scoping.

---

### Staff Recognition and Rewards

Beyond identifying problems, the HR manager wants the system to help identify and reward the best performers. This serves both a motivational purpose — staff who know they are being measured and recognised are more likely to perform consistently — and a retention purpose, acknowledging that good staff are worth keeping.

The mechanism discussed was vouchers and similar rewards, though the exact reward structure is Wendo's internal business decision rather than a system design question. What the system needs to do is surface top performers clearly — through performance data, positive supervisory assessments, and low disciplinary records — so that HR and management can make recognition decisions with confidence. Whether the system manages the reward distribution itself or simply informs the decision is a question for V2 scoping.

---

### Reporting

The HR manager expressed a strong interest in advanced reporting — both for HR specifically and across the broader system including inventory. The desire is for structured Daily, Weekly, and Monthly reports that give management a clear picture of what is happening at each branch and across the business as a whole.

V1 already includes reporting at the branch and director level. V2 will refine and expand this — improving the depth of existing reports and introducing HR-specific reporting such as attendance trends, disciplinary activity summaries, performance rankings, and supervisory assessment histories. The specific reports required across HR and inventory will be mapped out in detail during V2 scoping.

---

### Open Questions for V2 Planning

1. **Assessment form structure** — The format, frequency, and triggering conditions for supervisory assessments need to be defined. Are they submitted on a fixed schedule, after specific incidents, or both?
2. **Staff visibility into their own records** — Whether staff members can view their own performance data, assessments, and disciplinary history within the app needs to be decided, as it has implications for how sensitive information is handled.
3. **Warning issuance workflow** — Who has the authority to issue warnings within the system — HR manager only, or branch managers as well? Does a warning require approval before it is formally recorded?
4. **Recognition and reward distribution** — Whether the system manages reward distribution (e.g., generating vouchers) or simply identifies candidates for HR to act on externally.
5. **Report recipients and access** — Which reports are visible to which roles. For example, whether branch managers see HR reports for their branch only, or whether the HR manager has a cross-branch view.
6. **Integration with V1 performance data** — How historical V1 data (attendance, order counts, prep times) is carried forward and incorporated into V2 HR profiles.
