# Workforce slice 4a (Timesheets, overtime and requests): back-end decisions

Written 18 Oct 2026 on branch `feat/workforce-4a-timesheets` (cut from `feat/workforce-3-attendance`). Each row is a question Paper or the brief left open, what was built, why, and what changing it takes. Code: `backend/src/modules/workforce/timesheets/`. Chapters 8 and 9 of the approved Paper designs; Rules (Chapter 13) and Waiting for you (Chapter 15) are not in this piece.

## Decisions

| # | Question | Choice | Why | What changing it takes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Where does the cut-off live? Paper closes "on Friday 16 October" for a period ending Sunday 18, at 18:00. | **Changed 19 Oct 2026 (see M1 to M8).** Rule field `WEEK_AND_BREAKS.timesheetCutoff { daysAfterPeriodEnd: 2, time: '18:00' }`: the period closes 2 days AFTER its last day (October closes on 2 Nov at 18:00). Built first as `daysBeforePeriodEnd` for a four-week period; a stored value of that old shape is dropped on parse and the default applies. The Director edits it with the other week fields. | A cut-off with no home would be a constant in code. The rule keeps it a setting and the Rules screen (later) can show it. | Edit the default in `rules-defaults.ts`, or the rule version. |
| T2 | Which timesheet does a day after the cut-off belong to? | **SUPERSEDED 19 Oct 2026 (M1, M2).** It was: the next one, so Sat 17 and Sun 18 Oct counted in the 17 Oct to 13 Nov timesheet. Under a calendar month the period is the whole month and the cut-off falls after its last day, so no day moves between periods. | Nothing is lost or counted twice, and the closed report never changes after it closes. | `period-window.ts`. |
| T3 | What does "matches the rota" mean? | Every shift on the day: clocked in within the grace (LATENESS), clocked out within the grace of the scheduled end, nothing edited by a manager, the phone's clock right, not closed by the phone, nothing left open on a past day, and any time beyond the end fully covered by the service tail. Anything else is an exception with its reasons. | Matches Chapter 8: the manager sees only what needs a person. An edited day is always shown even if it now looks clean, so a fix is never invisible. | Reasons in `day-assess.ts`. |
| T4 | How are timesheets kept up to date? | Recomputed from the rota and the clock events when someone reads the period (at most every 20 seconds per period) and by the 10-minute job for every switched branch. Only changed rows are written, and one audit entry covers a batch of system approvals. | No hooks into the attendance code (another session works there). Reads stay cheap and a repeat run changes nothing (tested). | Call the refresh from an attendance event instead. |
| T5 | A person's decision versus the evidence moving underneath it. | A decision (approved, held, excused, overtime answer) stays while the day's evidence hash is unchanged. If a fix or a late clock-out changes it, an approved day goes back to "needs a look" with `CHANGED_AFTER_APPROVAL`; a day that now matches by itself becomes automatic. | The manager approved what they saw, not what it became. | `day-merge.ts`. |
| T6 | What happens to items still open when a period closes? | They move to the next period as `HELD` (days) and stay pending (overtime), marked "moved from the last period"; the close says how many. Nothing is silently approved or lost. | "Anything still open goes to HR" (Chapter 8). HR sees them first in the next period; the Waiting for you page that gathers them is a later piece. | `close-service.ts`. |
| T7 | Early close. | `POST /periods/:id/close` by the Branch or Store Manager (HR, System Admin): only on the period's last day or after; open items need `confirmCarry: true`. Closing "early" is not a way to skip anything. | Chapter 8 step 4: "this only brings it forward". | The `TOO_EARLY` check. |
| T8 | Who signs the report with a PIN, and when? | After the close, the Branch or Store Manager (`timesheet.close`) signs with their own PIN (`POST /periods/:id/sign`); the audit entry records `method: PIN` and the time, never the PIN. A wrong PIN is a 401; no lockout (the owner's rule for codes) and the tries are not logged here. | The automatic close at the cut-off cannot ask for a PIN, so the signature is its own step. The report shows who signed. | Add a try log if the owner wants one. |
| T9 | Closed period and attendance fixes. | A closed period refuses every fix, **HR included** (`PERIOD_CLOSED`); HR reopens the period with a reason first. This changes slice 3's hook, which let HR through. | The brief: "except HR's reopen path"; a reopen leaves a reason in the audit trail, a silent HR edit would not. | `period-lock.ts`. |
| T10 | Reopen. | HR only (`timesheet.reopen`). Clears the signature, sets a new cut-off the next day at the rule's time, records the reason; it closes again by itself. | Without a new cut-off it would be closed again by the next sweep at once. | `reopen-service.ts`. |
| T11 | Overtime: automatic part, limits and who decides. | The service tail (rule) is authorised by itself **only when open orders or tickets existed at the scheduled end**. The rest waits. Limits come from OVERTIME: a weekly cap per person and a budget per branch. Beyond either, a department head gets 403 `OVERTIME_NEEDS_MANAGER`; the Branch or Store Manager and HR decide, and must give a reason. A manager may authorise less than asked, never more. Approving authorises time only. | Proposal 5.5 and Chapter 8 step 3. | `overtime-plan.ts`. |
| T12 | Overtime evidence. | Counts only are stored (orders taken and tickets cleared in the extra time, open orders at the end). The takings of those orders are read for the card and not stored or used in any formula. | "Order value is never the pay formula." | `evidence-repository.ts`. |
| T13 | Excusing lateness. | Allowed for the LATENESS rule's `excuseWindowHours` (48) after the day; HR and the System Admin are not held to it. It marks the day excused (for the later deduction in slice 6); it changes no clock. "Adjust" is the existing attendance fix, not a second way to edit time. | Rule already exists; deductions are payroll's. | `decide-service.ts`. |
| T14 | Who a request first waits on. | The Branch Manager (the Store Manager for Store Attendants). Pay questions and a manager's own request go to HR. "Clocked out by mistake" is answered by the Branch or Store Manager and the System Admin, and by HR once it has moved there. | Chapter 9 and proposal section 6; nobody answers their own. | `requests-rules.ts`. |
| T15 | Escalation timing. | After 3 Nairobi calendar days with no answer, moved to HR once (a database claim, so two servers or two runs move it once). The proposal's 2 and 4 day reminders are not built. | Paper (approved after the proposal) says 3 days. | `ESCALATE_AFTER_DAYS`. |
| T16 | "Clocked out by mistake". | Takes the clock-out away so the shift is open again (or edits it to the real time if the person gave one). Applied through the attendance fix door as new correction rows; a retry finds the day already right and adds nothing. The person sees before and after. | Slice 3 left it for this slice (decision A11). | `clock-apply.ts`. |
| T17 | Report a problem scope. | Six kinds: clock time wrong, worked but not recorded, rota wrong, overtime missing, other pay, clocked out by mistake. Only clock kinds change time. One open request per kind and day. Days up to 45 days back. | Paper's five chips plus the mistake request. | Validators. |
| T18 | Visibility. | Staff: their own. Department head: their departments (head rows). Branch and Store Manager: their site. HR, Director, Accountant: all; the Accountant and the Director read and act on nothing. Other branch 404, a write without the capability 403. | The brief; one access table. | The table. |
| T19 | Weeks. | Weeks follow their days (cut at the period edges); there is no week sign-off. | Chapter 8 step 2: "No week sign-off needed". | n/a |
| T20 | Branches not switched on, and periods before switch-on. | No timesheets, nothing written, `enabled: false`. A period is created on the first read of the current period after the switch; earlier periods do not exist. | The brief: legacy stays untouched. | n/a |
| T21 | `getApprovedHours` for reporting and payroll. | Real now: approved minutes (system or person) and authorised overtime, never raw clock events. | The slice 0 stub named slice 4. | `approved-hours.ts`. |
| T22 | Not built, by the brief. | Payroll receipt on the report (`receivedByPayroll` is null until slice 6), notification delivery (log only), leave on the rota (slice 5), the Rules screens and Waiting for you. | Brief. | Later slices. |

## Calendar-month period (owner decision 19 Oct 2026)

| # | Question | Choice | Why | What changing it takes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | What is a timesheet period? | One calendar month, 1st to last day in Nairobi time. `CALENDAR_MONTH` is the default of `WEEK_AND_BREAKS.timesheetPeriod` (it already parsed); `FIXED_WEEKS` stays parseable so stored rules stay valid, but nothing uses it by default. A stored `FIXED_WEEKS` rule on a site still wins until someone edits it (none are stored on the lane database). | The owner's decision. | `rules-defaults.ts`, or edit the rule. |
| M2 | When does it close? | By itself at 18:00 Nairobi on the 2nd day after the month ends (`timesheetCutoff.daysAfterPeriodEnd` 2). It applies to any period kind. `period-window.ts` is now one function: window = the rule's period, cut-off = last day + 2 at 18:00. | So the last days are complete when a manager reviews. | The rule value. |
| M3 | Closing early. | Unchanged: only on the last day or after. | The brief keeps close early. | n/a |
| M4 | Part weeks (a Monday to Sunday week that runs into two months). | The week shows in each period with only that period's days. The week row is flagged `partWeek: true` in My time and the person view (computed from the week start against the period, no new column). | A flag computed on read cannot go stale. | `days/weeks.ts`. |
| M5 | Weekly rules and part weeks. | The weekly overtime cap reads the whole Monday to Sunday week: `refreshPeriod` also loads the days of the neighbouring period that share a week with this one (never later than today) and the plan adds their overtime to the week total. They are read-only for this period: nothing is written for them. Branch budget stays per period. | The cap is a rule about a week, not a month. | `assemble-service.ts`, `assemble-plan.ts` (`neighbourDays`). |
| M6 | Which period holds an overtime item that sits in a part week? | The period of the day the overtime happened on; a day belongs to exactly one period, so nothing moves between periods and a closed period is never touched. Each item records the whole week's total (`limits.weekMinutes`), so the item on or after the day the cap is crossed carries the flag in the period where that day falls. The brief's recommendation was "the period of the day the cap was crossed"; this keeps that period for the crossing day without moving earlier items. | Moving items between periods would touch closed ones. | `overtime-plan.ts` and the plan. |
| M7 | Existing data. | Migration `20261019100000_workforce_timesheet_calendar_month` (data only, no table or column changes) re-cuts every OPEN period that is not already a calendar month into the months it touches: days, exceptions, overtime items and weeks follow their date, requests are not touched (they hang on person and date). It stops with an error rather than lose a row. A CLOSED period is never changed, and the month after it starts the day after it (`periodsRepository.ensure` does the same for a window that overlaps an earlier period). The cut-off written is the rule default. Proved on a copy (`scripts/proof/timesheets-recut-proof.sh`): all other tables identical, second run changes nothing. The lane database had no periods yet. | Additive, repeatable, loses nothing. | A new migration. |
| M8 | Branch overtime budget per period. | Left as a per-period figure; a month is about as long as the old four weeks, so the value stays meaningful. | No owner figure to convert. | `overtime.branchBudgetMinutesPerPeriod`. |
| M9 | Week start in the re-cut. | The migration rebuilds the week rows with Monday as the week start (the default `weekStartsOn`). No site stores another value; the next change to a person's days rewrites their weeks with the site's own value. | No rule stored; SQL cannot read the rules store. | n/a |
