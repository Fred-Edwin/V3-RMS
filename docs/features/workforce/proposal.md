# Workforce — proposal (v2, approved approach)

Phase 3 of the design lane. Written 4 Oct 2026. Builds on `audit-and-critique.md`. This version records the owner's decisions from the review conversation of 4 Oct 2026. Nothing here is built.

**Placeholders.** Every number (grace minutes, allowances, multipliers, caps, reminder days) is an **example**. None is a legal or policy value. They become **settings** (section 11). The client's accountant confirms legal ones (overtime multipliers, statutory deductions). Deduction limits are for the accountant or the client's lawyer; the system makes them settings and keeps the evidence, it does not decide what is lawful.

## 1. The idea in one page

Today the rota, the clock and the payslip are three disconnected things and pay figures are typed by hand. The rebuild joins them in one chain and removes every place where a person types hours or pay.

```
 PLAN              FACT               REVIEWED TRUTH         MONEY
 Rota  ─────────►  Clock events ───►  Timesheet   ───────►   Pay run ───► Payslip
 (promise)         (what happened)    (manager-approved)     (calculated)
    ▲                                    ▲   │
    └── Leave ───────────────────────────┘   └── Overtime approval, lateness, corrections (each with a reason)
```

1. **The rota is the promise, the clock is the fact, the timesheet is the truth.** Payroll reads only approved timesheets.
2. **Manage by exception.** A clean day approves itself. Managers decide only late, left early, stayed on, missed clock, auto-closed, outside the location.
3. **Nothing is silent, lost or deleted.** Every clock event, correction, approval, deduction and pay change is a dated entry with who and why. Staff see their own hours and can report a problem with any line.

## 2. The people foundation (where the data starts)

### 2.1 Three things that today are mixed into one

| Concept | Meaning | Owned by | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | What the system lets you do (permissions) | Access & Organisation | Workforce reads **capabilities**, never role names (as Inventory does). Kitchen Display and Barista Display logins are **device accounts, not employees**. |
| **Position** | The job title: Head Barista, Cook, Waiter… | Workforce (`org`) | Editable list HR manages. Shown on the rota, payslip and file. |
| **Department** | Where you work in a branch: Kitchen, Pastry, Barista, Service, Housekeeping; Stores at the hub | Workforce (`org`) | A list of data. HR adds and edits departments as the company grows (a bakery, a delivery team). |

**Department head** is a *responsibility* held by one active employee per department per branch, not a role. The Branch Manager or HR assigns it in two clicks from the department card. The confirm summary says what the head will be able to do ("build the Pastry rota, approve overtime inside the allowance, see only their own pay"). Replacing a head shows the pending approvals being re-routed. While a head is on leave, their approvals go to the Branch Manager automatically. The `isDepartmentHead` and department claims other modules read stay available through Access; Workforce is the source of truth and tells Access when they change (boundary, section 3.4).

### 2.2 Who is tracked

An employee has a **"Tracks time"** setting, defaulted from the position, editable by HR.

| Tracks time (clock, timesheet, lateness, overtime) | Does not track time (salaried, no clock) |
|---|---|
| Waiter, Chef, Barista, Steward, Housekeeper, department heads, Store Attendant, Accountant, **casuals** (recorded by the head) | Branch Manager, Store Manager, Director, HR Manager, System Admin |

Non-tracking staff still have an employee file, leave, payslips and approvals; they have no clock card, timesheet, lateness or overtime. This removes "a manager's own overtime" from the approval chain.

### 2.3 Hire an employee (replaces three disconnected steps)

Today: a Branch Manager types an account (name, email, phone, role, temporary password) and an empty profile is created; HR later finds it at "0 of 13 details" with no contract; the Branch Manager assigns the department head on another screen; no pay is set up anywhere.

New **Add employee** flow, one guided screen sequence, five steps and a progress ring:

| Step | Contents | Who may fill |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Who | Name, phone, email, ID number, photo (optional now) | HR; Branch Manager (own branch) |
| 2 Job | Branch, department, position, role (access), reports to, start date, employment: **Monthly, with contract / Monthly, no contract yet / Casual (daily)**, tracks time | HR; Branch Manager (basics) |
| 3 Pay | Monthly amount or daily rate, overtime eligibility, lateness policy (inherits default) | HR, Director, Accountant (read/confirm) only; hidden from the Branch Manager |
| 4 Statutory and bank | KRA PIN, SHA, NSSF, HELB, bank or M-Pesa for payout | HR; the employee completes their own personal details later |
| 5 Login | Account, temporary password, first-sign-in PIN setup; **skipped for casuals** | HR (via Access) |

- **Onboarding checklist** on the file shows what is missing and who owns it. A person can be rostered and clock in as soon as steps 1–2 are done; payroll eligibility needs step 3 and 4. No half-created person.
- **No contract yet** uses a **default leave policy**, so balances exist (fixes today's dead end where leave cannot be requested).
- The employee completes their own details and photo on first sign-in (existing behaviour, kept); HR verifies.
- **Budget:** add a person with basics: 1 screen, 8 inputs or fewer; full record: 5 short steps.

### 2.4 Lifecycle

Onboarding → Active → On leave → Suspended → Notice → **Exited**. Exit is a flow, not a delete: last clock, leave balance, final pay run, return items, login deactivated; the record is kept. Transfer is a flow with a summary (open shifts moved or cancelled, balance carried, pay period split).

### 2.5 HR workspace

HR Home shows **what needs attention**: incomplete onboarding, people with no contract, probation ending, requests waiting for HR, payroll calendar. Below that: headcount by branch and department. The **directory** has card and table views, filters, search, and a command palette. The **employee file** has tabs: Overview, Job and pay, Time, Leave, Documents, Conduct, Activity. The **Organisation** view shows company → branch → department → head → members, with gaps flagged ("Pastry has no head").

### 2.6 Casuals (no login)

A casual is an employee file with no account, attached to a department's casual pool. Daily: the head ticks who worked and adjusts arrival and leave times; the Branch Manager approves; a printed **daily pay voucher** is produced; the casual signs on paper; the person paying signs with their PIN. Lateness for a casual reduces that day's payout and is shown on the voucher.

### 3.2 Dependencies (point downward, never loop)

```
rules ──────┬─► leave ─┐
employees ──┤          ├─► rota ─► attendance ─► timesheets ─► payroll
org ────────┘          │                              ▲           │
leave ─────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘           ▼
approvals (used by leave, timesheets, attendance, payroll, discipline, employees)   audit-log (every sub-module writes)
```

Each sub-module exposes a small `index.ts`; importing another's internals fails the build.

### 3.3 Public door (what other modules may call)

`getOnShiftNow(unit, role?)` (kitchen and barista displays, unchanged behaviour); `getEmployee(userId)`; `isOnLeave(userId, date)`; `getApprovedHours(userId, period)` (Reporting later). Events: `rota.published`, `clock.in`, `clock.out`, `overtime.requested`, `timesheet.approved`, `payroll.published`, `department.head_changed`.

### 3.4 Boundaries

- **Access & Organisation** keeps accounts, roles, branches/sites, sign-in and sessions. It emits sign-in, failed sign-in, password-change and session events for the audit log, and reads `department.head_changed` to refresh the claims other modules use. **Needs the Access lane's agreement.**
- **Orders and Fulfilment** are above Workforce. Workforce never imports them. They publish order and ticket events; Workforce keeps a small per-shift **activity snapshot** (orders handled, value, tickets cleared) used only as approval evidence. The "you still have open orders" content of the shift-end prompt is composed in the phone app.
- **Notifications** delivers pushes. Until the shared audit sink exists, `audit-log` owns a local table with the same shape.
- **Finance** will own petty cash; Workforce produces the daily voucher and payout totals only.

### 3.5 Where today's services go

`hr-service` → employees, leave, discipline; `staff-service` stays in Access (accounts); `staff-transfer-service` → employees; `department-service` → org; `shift-service` and `shift-assignment-service` → rota; `clock-service` → attendance; `payslip-service` → payroll; the 15-minute lateness constant → rules.

## 5. Flows and click budgets

Today's figure in brackets (measured or estimated, see the audit).

### 5.1 Rota (Branch Manager, Store Manager, department head, HR; HR also builds the Accountant's)

| Task | Budget | How |
|---|---|---|
| Start a week from last week | **2 clicks** (1 plus no preview) | Preview ("31 shifts, 2 clashes"), then Publish. Clashes are shown, not copied. |
| Set one shift | **2 clicks** (2 per cell) | Click the cell, click a shift chip. Keyboard letters and Delete. |
| Fill a row | **2 actions** | Pick a shift "brush", drag across the row. |
| Fresh 5-person week | **12 actions or fewer** (up to 70) | Brushes and drags, or one click on a standing pattern. |
| Change after publishing | **3 clicks** | Confirm summary names who is notified. |
| Head adds a day on a phone | **3 taps + 1 per person** | Keep today's day-at-a-time pattern, add a week overview. |

Warns, does not block: person on approved leave (override needs a reason), thin coverage, weekly hours above contract, a double shift. Always visible: hours per person for the week and people per shift per day; cost only for roles that may see pay. Past days are read-only: fixing the past happens in timesheets. No overnight shifts. No availability.

### 5.2 Clock and mistake-proofing (staff phone)

| Task | Budget | How |
|---|---|---|
| Clock in | **1 tap** (about 3) | Pinned card on Home; opens about 30 minutes before the shift (setting); only on a rostered shift. |
| Clock out | **1 tap** after scheduled end | Before the scheduled end it asks for a reason chip first. |
| Undo either | **1 tap, 2 minutes** (clock-out 60 s today) | Toast and card with a countdown ring; the shift continues with no gap. |
| Clocked out by mistake, after the window | **2 taps** | "I clocked out by mistake" goes to the Branch Manager **and** the System Admin; first to act resolves it. |
| See hours so far | **0 taps** | Live counter on the card; a "On shift 03:12" pill stays in the app header. |
| No signal or GPS trouble | **1 tap, then waits** | Tap is saved with its time and synced later; if no fix, plain words and "Ask my manager to confirm". Never a silent failure. |

Reliability: each tap carries a client id, so a double tap or retry cannot create two events. Device time and server time are both stored; a large difference is flagged, never trusted. The live counter uses the server's time.

### 5.3 Shift-end prompt (unavoidable)

- **10 minutes before:** a dismissible heads-up.
- **At the scheduled end:** a **full-screen prompt that cannot be closed without choosing** "Finish now" or "Stay on", repeated every 2 minutes with sound and vibration. If the app is closed, a push notification with the same two choices; reopening shows the prompt first.
- **Stay on** asks for a reason chip (rush service, waiting for guests, handover, closing duties) and an estimate (about 15, 30 or 60 minutes). It starts overtime as pending.
- **No answer in about 15 minutes:** the shift closes **at the scheduled end**, flagged; the head and Branch Manager are alerted; the next time the person opens the app, a blocking screen asks "Your shift was closed at 14:00. Was that right?".

| Role | The prompt also shows |
|---|---|
| Waiter | Open orders and tables ("Table 4, 2 items unpaid") |
| Chef, Barista | Tickets they claimed that are still open |
| Store Attendant | Pending picks, receiving and counts |
| Steward, Housekeeper | "Finished your tasks?" |
| Accountant | A simple "Finished for the day?" |

### 5.4 Manager's day (Today board and timesheets)

**Today board** (Branch Manager, Store Manager, head): who is in, late (minutes), not in yet, stayed on, mistaken-clock-out requests. One-tap actions: clock in for them (reason), mark absent, extend shift (pre-approves overtime), message.

| Task | Budget | How |
|---|---|---|
| Approve a normal week | **1 click** | "Approve all clean days (87)". |
| Decide an exception | **1–2 clicks** | Scheduled vs actual as a small timeline bar; accept, excuse or adjust with a reason chip. |
| Fix a missed clock-out, any day in an open period | **3 clicks** (today only) | Set time, pick reason, save. |
| Close the period | **1 click + confirm** | Blocks and names unreviewed exceptions. |

### 5.5 Overtime (plain version)

1. Overtime is time worked **after your shift ends**. It is **paid only if approved**.
2. It can be approved three ways: **before** (the manager extends the shift), **after** (you tap Stay on and the manager approves), or **automatically by the service tail**.
3. The **service tail**: a small allowance (about 15 to 30 minutes, a setting) recognised automatically **only if the system can see open orders or tickets at shift end**. It lets staff finish serving guests with no form. Beyond it, a manager decides. No open work means no automatic recognition.
4. The approval card shows **evidence**: orders handled, their value, tables still open, tickets cleared in the extra window. "No activity recorded" makes declining the obvious choice.
5. **Limits:** a weekly cap per person and an overtime budget per branch, both settings; unusual patterns (same person always, at slow hours) are flagged in a report.
6. **Pay** is hours times a multiplier from the rules, confirmed by the Accountant. Order value is **never** the pay formula (it invites gaming and is unfair between slow and busy hours). A performance incentive, if the client wants one, is a separate decision later.
7. Nobody approves their own. Unapproved time is recorded, shown to the staff member as pending, not paid and never deleted; it goes to the next approver after the window.

Approver: department head for their own department (inside the allowance), otherwise Branch Manager (Store Manager at the hub), then HR.

### 5.6 Lateness and deductions

1. **Record** lateness after a **grace period** (example 5 minutes).
2. The manager has a window to **excuse** it, with a reason chip.
3. The **policy** (set by the Director or Branch Manager, D11): record only; deduct after N lates in a month; or deduct from the first minute after grace. Optionally "make up the time the same day".
4. The staff member sees the **estimated deduction as it builds up** and can Report a problem before payroll.
5. A **cap** per pay period (setting). A wrong deduction is **reversed in the next run** as a visible line.
6. Missing a whole shift is deducted **only when the manager marks it "unexcused"**, never automatically.
7. Deduction = minutes times the person's rate (monthly pay divided by scheduled paid minutes in the period; daily staff: the day's rate, scaled). It is a pay line with its working shown.

### 5.7 Report a problem (replaces "Query")

Staff pick a line and tap **Report a problem**: what is wrong (wrong time, missed clock-out, lateness not fair, overtime not counted, other), the correct time if they know it, a note. The approver sees **recorded vs requested** side by side and **accepts** (the timesheet changes, with an audit entry) or **declines with a reason**. The staff member sees the status ("Waiting on Faith Njeri") and the outcome. This is the same pattern Deputy and Homebase use (the employee proposes a change with a reason; the manager compares and approves or rejects). **Follow-up:** reminder after 2 days; after 4 days it moves to the next person who can act (D12); if the approver is the subject of the report, it goes straight to HR. A **My requests** list shows everything open, with who it waits on.

### 5.8 Leave

Staff request in 4 taps (dates on one calendar, type with balance shown, send; reason only when policy requires it). The head sees clashes; the Branch Manager approves (a manager's own leave goes to HR). Approved leave blocks the rota cell and counts as scheduled paid hours. **Default leave policy** applies until a contract is assigned.

### 5.9 Payroll

| Task | Budget | How |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare a monthly run | **1 click** when hours are approved | Reads approved timesheets and pay profiles. |
| Review | per exception, 1 click | Only unusual lines: change vs last month, new joiner, leaver, missing pay profile, unapproved overtime. |
| Approve | **1 click** (Accountant) | The preparer cannot approve their own run. |
| Publish | **1 click + confirm summary** (HR or Director) | Staff see payslips **only after publishing** (today they see drafts live). |
| Reopen | **2 clicks + reason**, Director | Audited, staff notified. |
| Casual payout | per day, 3 clicks | Head records, Branch Manager approves, voucher prints. |

Each payslip line **shows its working** ("Overtime: 3 h 40 min × rate × multiplier, rule version 4"). Statutory deductions: the system **calculates** them from dated tables the Accountant enters and confirms; until confirmed, payroll runs in "statutory typed" mode with a banner (decision pending with the Accountant).

## 7. Time zone and timers

**Finding (verified by running the code):** production runs in UTC. The attendance and lateness code uses the server's local time. For a 06:00 Nairobi shift and a 06:05 clock-in, it computes **175 minutes early** on a UTC server (5 minutes late on a Nairobi-time machine). Four places in the HR repository, and the leave working-days count, are affected. Lateness reports on production are very likely wrong; my lane runs on Nairobi time, which is why the demo looked right.

**Fix (part of the build):** every moment stored in UTC; shift times are **Nairobi clock times**; all workforce date and time maths goes through one Nairobi helper (one already exists); tests run with the server set to another zone; the phone's live counter uses server time so a wrong phone clock cannot change hours.

## 8. Audit and security

**Two views:** the **company log** (limited by role below) and **Activity on my record** for every employee.

**Logged:** sign-ins, failed sign-ins and password or PIN changes (from Access); every payslip unlock (success and failure) and every payslip open, with who, when, device and approximate location; views of pay, ID and bank details; every change to hours, leave, pay, rules, roles, departments and heads, with before, after and reason; every approval and signature; every export or print. Entries are **append-only** and tamper-evident; nobody, including the System Admin, can edit or delete. **Who looked at the log is itself logged.** A payslip entry shows that it was opened, never the amounts. Each entry is written **in the same database step as the change**, so a change cannot happen without a trace.

**For the employee:** "Payslip June 2026 opened · today 14:05 · Android, Nyeri" with a **"This wasn't me"** button: it signs out all sessions, forces a password change, and alerts the Director. An **instant alert** is sent when their payslip is opened by someone else, or from an unrecognised device. Wrong-password attempts at the gate lock it briefly and alert.

| Role | Sees in the audit log |
|---|---|
| Staff | Only **Activity on my record** |
| Department head | No log; the history of the item in front of them |
| Branch Manager, Store Manager | Their own branch or hub: rota, clock corrections, overtime, leave, timesheets. Not pay, payslip opens, discipline |
| HR | People and time events company-wide and pay-run preparation. Not payslip opens or sign-in security events |
| Accountant | Pay-run approvals, statutory and pay-rule changes, payslip publishing |
| Director | Everything, read-only, including **who opened whose payslip** |
| System Admin | Account and security events and rule changes. Not payslip opens |

Principle: the people being watched do not read the watching.

## 10. Premium design direction

- **Language:** Geist and Geist Mono, warm neutrals, espresso primary, caramel accent, 2 px radii, from the approved tokens. **Colour carries meaning only:** on time green, late amber, overtime caramel, absent red, pending a quiet blue-grey.
- **One hero per screen:** the clock card is a large live time; the rota is the week; timesheets lead with the exceptions count; payroll leads with the net total.
- **Numbers:** time and money in tabular Geist Mono, right aligned, consistent units. **Time is drawn:** scheduled vs actual as small timeline bars.
- **Surfaces over boxes:** information sits on surfaces with hairline dividers, not nested cards. No Excel look: no blue bars, sheet tabs or "Export to Excel" as the interface.
- **Motion with a reason** (the emil-design-eng principles: explain cause and effect, fast, no motion on frequent keyboard actions, respect reduced motion): about 120–200 ms ease-out; the clock button fills as it is pressed and the counter starts; undo shows a countdown ring; a shift chip drops in; an approved row collapses; optimistic updates; skeletons not spinners; haptics on clock actions.
- **Keyboard-first on desktop:** a command palette, shortcuts shown in tooltips, visible focus rings.
- **Phone:** thumb-zone bottom sheets, targets of 48 px or more, one primary action, no sideways scrolling.
- **Empty states lead to the next action.** Plain language, names not codes, reason chips. Warm greetings ("Good morning, Brian. Morning shift starts at 06:00").
- **Documents** follow the LPO: A4, cursive signature, QR.
- **Photos** replace avatars everywhere (rota, clock card, approvals, file); initials until uploaded.

Every change creates a **new version from a chosen date**, never edits history, and appears in the audit log with the previous value; the other party (Director or Branch Manager) is notified.

## 12. Alternatives rejected

Hard clock-out with no prompt (deletes real work); overtime paid by order value (gaming, unfair, likely not lawful as a pay basis); editing hours inside payroll (untraceable); restyling the spreadsheet only (the cost is the 70 actions); one rota screen per role (four copies); the Bradford Factor absence score (ignores reasons, penalises genuine illness); a separate casual role (the head already supervises them); escalating to read-only roles (they can see but cannot act); designing by role or by screen (hides the hand-overs).

## 13. How the designs are organised

**Chapters by job, steps tagged by role and device**, as Inventory's chapters ("Step 9 · Anyone · Print the LPO"). A chapter follows one job across the people who touch it. At the front: a **role index** (role × chapter) and a **screens index**. At the back: **When things go wrong**, one **states kit** (loading, empty, error, offline) with a copy table, and a **wording table** for System Admin signing. One artboard, **Workforce · Interaction spec**, tells the builder how each thing behaves (trigger, response, timing and easing, every state, offline, undo windows, keyboard).

| Group | Chapters (in design order) |

| **A. The people** | 1 Role homes and navigation · 2 Hire an employee · 3 Departments and heads · 4 Employee file and HR workspace |

Signed documents appear as the last step of the chapter that creates them. Each chapter gets its own Paper page; the owner approves it, and the approval is recorded in the sub-module README under `docs/features/workforce/`, before the next starts. Group A is designed first because everything rests on who the people are.
